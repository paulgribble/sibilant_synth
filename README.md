# sibilant_synth

A data-driven MATLAB synthesizer for **she / see / shoe / sue** tokens whose
sibilant lies anywhere on a continuum from /ʃ/ (`a = 0.0`) to /s/ (`a = 1.0`),
built from the recordings of the `sibilant_experiment` cohort (72 talkers).
Intended for a two-alternative forced-choice perception experiment
("did you hear *she* or *see*?").

```matlab
[y, Fs, info] = SynthSibilant(0.35, "i");              % random talker, she-ish
[y, Fs, info] = SynthSibilant(1.0,  "u", "pert6P02");  % "sue" from one talker
sound(y, Fs)
SynthSibilantTalkers()                                 % the 72 talker ids
WriteSibilantContinuum("stimuli", 'A', 0:0.1:1)        % WAVs + manifest.tsv (stimuli/ is gitignored)
```

`a` is the continuum position, `vowel` is `"i"` (she/see) or `"u"`
(shoe/sue), and the optional third argument is a talker id or `"random"`.
Name-value options: `Seed` (reproducible noise, talker and template choice),
`Template` (which of the talker's two excitation templates), `Level` (vowel
RMS in dBFS, default −20), `PadMs`, `SibDur` (override the sibilant
duration), `Play`, `Model`. `info` returns the talker, durations, sample
indices of the sibilant and vowel, and the sibilant's peak frequency per
time slice.

`poly2lsf`, `lsf2poly`). The synthesizer only needs the `.mat`; the raw data

## How the model is built (`BuildSibilantModel`)

For each participant of every cohort except `ryan` (no practice block, so no
see/sue), every **unshifted** trial (practice and baseline blocks) of the four
words is analysed from the microphone channel between the hand-scored
boundaries (`scored_data/<pid>_scored.tsv`). Per talker × word:

- **Sibilant spectrum**: multitaper spectra (40 ms, 7 tapers) at 5 equally
  spaced points across the sibilant, averaged over trials in linear power,
  placed on a 1/48-octave grid (100 Hz–22.05 kHz) and lightly smoothed
  (σ = 1/24 octave). Five slices capture the within-sibilant dynamics, e.g.
  the anticipatory lip rounding before /u/.
- **Spectral landmark** per slice: the power-weighted log-frequency centroid
  of the region within 6 dB of the in-band maximum (2–5 kHz for /ʃ/, 3–10 kHz
  for /s/). This is what the morph aligns.
- **Sibilant RMS envelope** (25 points over normalised time), **duration**
  (median) and **level relative to the following vowel** (mean dB).
- **Vowel filter**: order-40 LPC (pre-emphasis 0.97, 30 ms windows) in 40
  normalised-time frames, converted to line spectral frequencies (LSF) and
  averaged over trials in the LSF domain (which keeps formant bandwidths,
  unlike averaging spectra). **RMS contour**, duration and mid-vowel F0 are
  stored alongside.
- **Excitation templates** per talker × vowel: the LPC residuals of the two
  she/shoe recordings whose vowel duration is closest to the median.

Trials with clipping, sibilants < 60 ms or vowels < 80 ms are dropped;
for words with ≥ 10 trials, duration and level outliers (> 3 robust SD) are
dropped too. `pert6P08` is excluded (no usable *see* trials). Practice and
baseline give 30 she, 30 shoe, 5 see and 5 sue trials per talker.

## How a token is synthesised (`SynthSibilant`)

**Sibilant.** White noise is shaped frame by frame (STFT, 1024-point frames,
75 % overlap) by a time-varying envelope. At `a = 0` and `a = 1` the envelope
is the talker's mean /ʃ/ or /s/ spectrum *in the requested vowel context*
(she vs shoe, see vs sue). In between, the two spectra are **morphed rather
than mixed**: the frequency axis is warped piecewise-linearly **on the mel
scale** so that the two landmarks coincide at the interpolated position
`mel⁻¹((1−a)·mel(f_ʃ) + a·mel(f_s))`, and the levels are then interpolated
bin by bin. Equal steps in `a` are therefore equal steps in mel
(mel = 2595·log10(1 + f/700)), i.e. perceptually equal steps in frequency;
`info.sibPeakHz` reports the resulting landmark per slice. The main energy
region thus glides continuously from the /ʃ/ frequency to the /s/ frequency
and the intermediate spectra stay unimodal, instead of the double-peaked
spectra a simple cross-fade would give. Duration (log-linear), RMS envelope
and level relative to the vowel are interpolated with `a` as well.

**Vowel.** The talker's LSF trajectory after /ʃ/ and after /s/ are
interpolated with weight `a` (frame by frame) and converted back to an
all-pole filter that is updated every 5 ms and driven by one of the talker's
own LPC residuals, then de-emphasised. Pitch, voice quality and breathiness
are therefore the talker's own, while the vocal-tract filter, including the
coarticulatory difference between the vowel of *she* and of *see* (mainly
the formant onsets), moves with the sibilant. The RMS contour is
interpolated too. Vowel duration and F0 are fixed by the excitation
template. The sibilant and vowel are joined with a 10 ms cross-fade, and the
vowel RMS is set to `Level` dBFS with the sibilant at its data-driven level
relative to it.

## Validation

`TestSynthSibilant` and `ValidateSynthSibilant` measure the synthetic
sibilant exactly as the experiment measures the real ones (three 50 ms
multitaper windows at mid-sibilant) and compare **whole level-normalised
spectra** (1/12 octave, 500 Hz–16 kHz) with the per-trial spectra in
`extracted_data/participants/*_spectra.tsv`:

- **Endpoint fit**: RMS over frequency bins of (synthetic − real mean)/real
  SD; a real trial scores ≈ 0.7–0.9 by construction (leave-one-out). The
  synthetic endpoints score 0.53 (/ʃ/) and 0.63 (/s/) on median and lie
  below the real trials' median in 98 % of the 144 talker × vowel cells:
  they sit inside the talker's own trial cloud, closer to its mean than a
  typical real trial.
- **Continuum position**, two views. (i) On each talker's own /ʃ/→/s/
  spectral axis (0 = that talker's real /ʃ/ mean, 1 = real /s/ mean) the
  position runs from −0.02 to 0.97 (medians), is monotonic in 100 % of cells
  and moves 0.08–0.12 per 0.1 step in `a`. (ii) A Fisher discriminant
  trained on all 4,972 real trials (99.5 % correct on the real trials) puts
  the endpoints at 0.04 and 1.02 with a mildly S-shaped mean course; on this
  population axis individual curves wobble by a few hundredths, less than
  the within-talker spread of real trials (SD ≈ 0.1).
- **Vowel**: mid-vowel F1/F2 of the synthetic endpoints vs the talker's
  real means (`all_extracted.tsv`): median error −3/−2 Hz after /ʃ/ and
  −7/−1 Hz after /s/ (MAD ≤ 37 Hz). `TestSynthSibilant` also overlays the
  F1/F2 tracks against the real mean tracks, including the she–see
  difference at vowel onset.

See `test_output/fig/` for the figures and `test_output/wav/` for listening.
Run `ValidateSynthSibilant` for the all-talker summary (printed and written
to `test_output/validation_all.tsv`). `test_output/` is not tracked in git
(see `.gitignore`); run `TestSynthSibilant` and `ValidateSynthSibilant` to
regenerate it after cloning.

## Known limitations

- Vowel duration and F0 come from the excitation template, so they do not
  vary with `a`; only the vocal-tract filter and RMS contour do.
- Only 5 see and 5 sue trials exist per talker, so the /s/ endpoints and the
  post-/s/ vowel filters rest on fewer trials than the /ʃ/ side.
- All participants are flagged `male` in `participants.tsv`; the gender only
  affects the formant-tracking presets used in validation, not the synthesis.
- The morph aligns one landmark (the main energy region). Secondary features
  such as the /s/ peak near 11 kHz appear or fade by level interpolation
  rather than gliding.
